What you can offer — and what it costs you

A larger deposit
Signals commitment. Trade-off: more cash committed before your loan is approved.
Appraisal gap coverage
You cover up to a set amount if the appraisal comes in low. Trade-off: you need that cash available.
An escalation clause
Your offer beats others up to a cap. Trade-off: it reveals your ceiling.
Informational-only inspection
Stronger than a full contingency, safer than waiving. I rarely recommend waiving entirely.
A flexible closing date
Matching the seller's timeline costs little and can be worth more than money.

Strategies

The same property four ways: buy & hold, house hack, BRRRR and fix & flip.

Estimate only — confirm with your lender and CPA.

When each one makes sense

Buy & hold
Buy, rent and hold for years. Makes sense when cash flow is positive after real reserves and you want slow, steady wealth. Risk: vacancies and major repairs.
House hack
Live in one unit and rent the others, with owner-occupant financing (FHA 3.5% or conventional 5%). Makes sense for first-time buyers: tenants pay much of your mortgage. You must live there at least a year.
BRRRR — buy, rehab, rent, refinance, repeat
Buy below value, renovate, rent, then refinance at the new value to pull your cash back out. Makes sense when the renovation creates real value. Risk: cost overruns and a low appraisal.
Fix & flip
Buy, renovate and sell. The 70% rule: pay no more than 70% of the after-repair value, minus the rehab. Risk: time, holding costs and the market when you sell.
