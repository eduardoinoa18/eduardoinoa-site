What selling your home actually costs in Massachusetts and New Hampshire.

Price is what the market says; net is what you keep. The short list of costs between the two, with real numbers.

Eduardo Inoa, REALTOR®The Fermin Group · Century 21 North East

The question I get most from sellers is not "what is my home worth" — it is "what will I actually keep?" The answer is the sale price minus a short list of costs. Here is that list, so nothing on your closing statement is a surprise.

1. Paying off what you owe

Your mortgage balance, any home-equity line, and a small payoff fee from the lender. The payoff letter is ordered before closing, and interest runs to the day of closing — so the number is a little higher than your last statement.

2. Commission

My listing fee is negotiable and in writing before we go to market. Separately, you decide whether to offer compensation to the buyer's agent. Offering it usually widens the pool of buyers; it is your choice, and we discuss it with real numbers before you sign anything.

3. State transfer stamps

StateWho paysHow much
MassachusettsSeller$4.56 for every $1,000 of the sale price (about $2,280 on a $500,000 sale)
New HampshireSplit buyer / seller$15 per $1,000 total — $7.50 per $1,000 from each side

4. Attorney, prorations and the small lines

  • Your attorney prepares the deed and clears title. Budget a few hundred to roughly a thousand dollars depending on the file.
  • Prorations: property taxes, water and sewer, and condo fees are split to the closing date. Sometimes they come back to you as a credit.
  • Smoke and carbon-monoxide certificate (Massachusetts), final water reading, and recording fees.

What this looks like on a real sale

On a $500,000 sale in Massachusetts with a $300,000 mortgage balance, the typical seller walks away with somewhere around $165,000 to $175,000 after everything above — the exact figure depends on the commission you agree to and what the buyer negotiates.
