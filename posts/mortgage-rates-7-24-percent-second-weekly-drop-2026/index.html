Mortgage rates fall to 7.24%: what the second drop means for you.

Mortgage rates in Massachusetts and NH fell to 7.24% this week, a second drop. What 0.16 points does to a payment, and what buyers and sellers can do.

Eduardo Inoa, REALTOR®The Fermin Group · Century 21 North East
Read in Spanish
In short
  • Freddie Mac's 30-year fixed rate fell to 7.24% on October 29, the second weekly decline, from 7.32% and a 7.40% peak.
  • On a $400,000 loan, that is about $44 a month less than at 7.40%, but still about $284 more than a year ago.
  • A few cents of movement matters less than the home, the price and the terms you negotiate.
  • Ask your lender about a fresh pre-approval, a seller credit or buydown, and your rate lock.

Mortgage rates fell again this week. Freddie Mac's survey put the average 30-year fixed rate at 7.24% on October 29, below 7.32% the week before and 7.40% in the two weeks before that. It is the second weekly decline in a row, and the lowest reading of the month. If you are watching mortgage rates in Massachusetts and Southern New Hampshire, here is what the move is worth in dollars and what it leaves unchanged.

What mortgage rates in Massachusetts did this month

The national average peaked at 7.40% on October 8 and again on October 15, then eased in each of the last two weeks. A year ago, on October 30, 2025, the same survey stood at 6.17%, so rates are still well above where they were.

Two weeks of decline is a pause in a climb, not a new trend, and no one can say where the next reading will land. What I can tell you is what each step is worth to a monthly payment.

What a 0.16-point drop does to a payment

As an illustration, take a $400,000 loan on a 30-year fixed mortgage, principal and interest only. At 7.40% the payment is about $2,770 a month. At 7.24% it is about $2,726, roughly $44 less. At last year's 6.17% it was about $2,442, which means today's payment is still about $284 higher than a year ago. Put another way, the $2,770 payment that carried $400,000 at 7.40% carries about $406,000 at 7.24%.

That is real money over a year, about $530, but it will not change which homes you can afford by itself. You can run your own numbers in the payment calculator in my buyer toolkit or see how much house fits your budget with the affordability tool.

If you are already under contractAsk your lender whether your rate is locked, for how long, and whether there is a float-down option. A lock protects you when rates rise, and a float-down lets you take part when they fall. Know which one you have before the next Thursday survey comes out.

What this means for buyers

  • Do not time the week. A move of a few cents changes your payment by tens of dollars, while the right home, price and terms change it by hundreds. Decide the monthly payment you are comfortable with and shop inside it.
  • Rerun your pre-approval. A letter written when rates were at 7.40% understates what you may qualify for today, and one from the summer overstates it. Ask your lender for a fresh one before you write an offer.
  • Ask for a credit or a buydown. A seller credit toward closing costs or a temporary rate buydown can lower your first-year payment more than the same dollars off the price. Have your lender show both side by side.
  • Remember that you can refinance later. If rates fall after you buy, a refinance is possible, but it is not guaranteed and it has closing costs. Buy on the payment you can carry today.

What this means for sellers

  • Buyers shop by monthly payment. At 7.24% the same budget reaches a slightly higher price than it did two weeks ago, but not as high as it did last year. Price your home for the payment buyers can actually make.
  • Consider offering a credit. Money toward a buyer's rate or closing costs often helps their payment more than a price cut of the same size, and your list price stays where it is.
  • Presentation matters more when budgets are tight. Strong photos, a clean inspection story and clear pricing from day one help a home stand out in towns like Haverhill and Lowell.
  • If you are also buying, a lower rate on your next home is a help, but plan the timeline carefully. Here are four ways to sell and buy at once.

What this means for investors

For an investor, the rate is one input among several. A 0.16-point drop moves the debt payment on a $400,000 loan by about $44 a month, which matters far less than the rent, the taxes and the repairs on the building. If you are looking at a two- or three-family, start with the numbers in the five numbers that decide a first multi-family and test the deal at today's rate and at a rate higher than that.

A lower rate helps the payment. The right home at the right price still decides the deal.

Questions people are asking

What is the mortgage rate in Massachusetts this week?

Freddie Mac's national weekly average for a 30-year fixed mortgage was 7.24% on October 29, 2026, down from 7.32% the week before. Your own rate depends on your credit, down payment and lender, so ask for a quote.

How much does a drop to 7.24% save on a mortgage payment?

On a $400,000 loan, principal and interest is about $2,726 a month at 7.24% compared with about $2,770 at 7.40%, roughly $44 less, before taxes and insurance. This is an illustration, not a loan quote.

Should I wait for rates to fall further before buying?

No one can say where rates will go. Waiting has a cost if prices or competition rise, and buying has a cost if the payment stretches you. Choose a payment you can carry today; refinancing later is possible, but not guaranteed, and has closing costs.
