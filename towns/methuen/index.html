The short version

Methuen, briefly

Methuen sits on the New Hampshire border, wrapped around the north side of Lawrence, with Haverhill to the east and Dracut to the west. I-93 begins its Massachusetts run here and I-495 crosses the eastern side of the city, so for a lot of my clients it is the practical middle of the Merrimack Valley: quick to the highways, quick to Salem, NH, and a short drive to the Lawrence train station.

It has a mayor–council city government, though you will still hear plenty of people call it “the town.” My office is a few minutes away in Lawrence, and Methuen is one of the places I work most.

Housing

What the housing stock looks like

Methuen’s housing is varied, which is a big part of its appeal. Closer to the Lawrence line and around the older center you will find two- and three-family homes and older single-families on smaller lots. Further out, mid-century capes, ranches and split-levels give way to colonials, newer subdivisions and condominium communities.

That range lets a buyer trade lot size against commute, and it gives investors owner-occupied multi-family options without leaving a suburban city. On older homes, we plan the inspection around heating systems, electrical, roofs and — for any home built before 1978 — lead paint.

Getting around

Getting to and from Methuen

Check the MBTA and MassDOT or NHDOT for current schedules and roadwork.

  • By train

    There is no station in Methuen. Most commuters drive to Lawrence station on the MBTA Haverhill Line, or to Bradford or Haverhill, for service to Boston’s North Station.

  • By car

    I-93 has exits in Methuen and I-495 crosses the east side of the city; Route 213, the Loop Connector, links the two. Routes 28, 110 and 113 handle local trips.

  • Across the line

    Salem and Pelham, New Hampshire, border Methuen to the north, so shopping and work in Southern New Hampshire are a short drive.

Schools

Schools in Methuen

Methuen’s public schools are run by the Methuen Public School District. For school assignments, enrollment and programs, go straight to the district — it is the only source I point clients to.

I don't rank or rate schools; the district and the state are the sources to trust. The Massachusetts Department of Elementary and Secondary Education publishes school and district profiles for every public district in the state.

Good to know

A few things about Methuen

  1. Methuen Memorial Music Hall

    A concert hall built to house a large pipe organ, and one of the city’s best-known landmarks.

  2. Forest Lake

    A recreational great pond of a little over 55 acres in the city.

  3. Nevins Memorial Library

    The public library building was completed in 1883. The nearby Searles Tenney Nevins Historic District was established in 1992.

  4. Tenney State Park

    A small state park within the city.

Is there a train from Methuen to Boston?

Not inside Methuen. Most people drive to Lawrence station on the MBTA Haverhill Line, or to Bradford or Haverhill, for service to North Station. Check the MBTA schedule for current times and parking.

What kinds of homes are in Methuen?

A wide mix: two- and three-family homes and older single-families near the Lawrence line and the older center, and capes, ranches, split-levels, colonials and condominiums further out.

Can my first home in Methuen be a two- or three-family?
