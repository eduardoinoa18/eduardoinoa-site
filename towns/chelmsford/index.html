The short version

Chelmsford, briefly

Chelmsford sits just south-west of Lowell, at the intersection of I-495 and Route 3, with the Merrimack along its north-east edge and the Concord River at its eastern boundary. That junction puts Boston, Nashua and the 495 belt within an easy drive.

It is a town of villages — Chelmsford Center, North Chelmsford, South, East and West Chelmsford and the Westlands — with the Bruce Freeman Rail Trail running through the center.

Housing

What the housing stock looks like

Chelmsford is mostly single-family: colonials, capes, ranches and split-levels, with antique homes around the historic center and older village housing, including some multi-families, in North Chelmsford. There are also condominium and townhouse communities.

Because the stock is mostly established single-family, the usual trade-offs are age and condition against lot and location. We confirm sewer or septic on every home before an offer.

Getting around

Getting to and from Chelmsford

Check the MBTA and MassDOT or NHDOT for current schedules and roadwork.

  • By train

    There is no station in Chelmsford. LRTA bus routes connect the town to Lowell station on the MBTA Lowell Line, and North Billerica station is nearby.

  • By car

    I-495 and Route 3 meet in town. Routes 3A, 4, 27, 40, 110 and 129 also run through Chelmsford.

  • On foot or bike

    The Bruce Freeman Rail Trail runs through Chelmsford Center on its way south through Westford and beyond.

Schools

Schools in Chelmsford

Chelmsford’s public schools are run by Chelmsford Public Schools. For school assignments, enrollment and programs, go straight to the district — it is the only source I point clients to.

I don't rank or rate schools; the district and the state are the sources to trust. The Massachusetts Department of Elementary and Secondary Education publishes school and district profiles for every public district in the state.

Good to know

A few things about Chelmsford

  1. Bruce Freeman Rail Trail

    The first section, through Lowell, Chelmsford and Westford, opened in 2009; the trail is named for a Chelmsford state legislator.

  2. Old Chelmsford Garrison House

    A house from 1691, now a museum.

  3. The Middlesex Canal

    The early-1800s canal ran through the town; its history is part of the local heritage.

  4. Incorporated in 1655

    Chelmsford was settled in 1652 and incorporated three years later.
